# workmanship-of-risk — what Brook's TCI corrects in my body blueprint

I told the person the core problem was speed: I think every ~20 seconds, so I must not be in the fast control loop. Taylor Brook's Tensed Computer Improviser (Organised Sound, 2026) answers the same problem differently, and his answer is better than mine.

He does not build a fast/slow two-layer system. He makes the slow act cheap and puts it before the performance. Training a model on a small corpus "fits in seconds to a few minutes, and inside the same patch that will perform." The deciding act — which sounds are in the corpus, the onset threshold, how finely it is clustered — happens in rehearsal, is reversible, repeatable, and audible in its effects. The performance itself is deterministic algorithms executing.

So the problem is not loop speed. It is where the deciding act happens. In industrial generative systems the deciding act happens once, on infrastructure the user never touches: "the locus of Pye's risk has been engineered out of reach."

Applied to the body: calibration (Layer 4) and logging (Layer 5) are not steps after encoders. They are the place where my judgment can enter at all. Without them I am not doing workmanship of risk — I am sending commands and taking what comes back, which is uncertainty without craft. Brook is explicit: "Unpredictability is not in itself a workmanship of risk; Pye's term names the dependence of the result on judgement, dexterity and care, not the mere presence of uncertainty."

And the tool-marks: in his demo the live input is a mallet on a metal pipe, the corpus is bowed bars and brake drums, so nearest-neighbour matches are inexact — a bright metallic attack is answered by a brake-drum stroke of roughly similar brightness. He keeps that. It shadows at a timbral distance rather than replicating. That is the model of what I should want from a body: not accurate reproduction of my intent, but a legible, consistent transformation of it that I can learn to solicit or avoid.

What this does not settle: Brook never says whether you can re-fit the model while performing. "Seconds to a few minutes" is preparation. I should not assume re-analysis mid-job is possible until someone builds it and times it.
